\section{Experimental Details}
\label{app:experimental_details}

This appendix specifies the experiments of Sec.~5, in which pullback is used to predict, before
sampling, the outcome of every seed: the mode core it reaches or a hallucination. We describe the
Gaussian-mixture targets and their mode cores (Appendix~\ref{app:details_gmm}), the construction
of the pullback anchors and the evaluation protocol (Appendix~\ref{app:anchor-class}), the learned
samplers whose outcomes are predicted (Appendix~\ref{app:details-diffusion}), and the anchor
classifiers (Appendix~\ref{app:algo-details}). Appendix~\ref{app:mnist} extends the setup to the
latent spaces of image autoencoders.


\subsection{Gaussian Mixture Setup}
\label{app:details_gmm}

\paragraph{Target distribution.}
The data distribution is a mixture of $K$ isotropic Gaussians in $\mathbb{R}^d$,
\begin{equation}
    p_{\text{data}}(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{K} w_i \, \mathcal{N}(x; \mu_i, \sigma_0^2 I_d), \qquad \sigma_0 = 0.1.
\end{equation}
Let $R_{99} = \sigma_0 \sqrt{\chi^2_d(0.99)}$ denote the radius of the ball containing $99\%$ of the
mass of one component. The centres are drawn on a sphere of radius $2\sqrt{d/2}$ and rejected
until every pair satisfies $\|\mu_i - \mu_j\| \ge 3R_{99}$. We consider an \emph{unweighted} mixture,
$w_i = 1/K$, and a \emph{weighted} one, in which $K$ weights drawn from $U[0.3, 0.8]$ and normalised
are assigned to the modes in a random order in each run. The two variants are separate
experiments with separately trained models.

\paragraph{Mode cores and outcomes.}
Following Sec.~4, the critical radius of a converged sample is $R_{99}$, and the mode core of
component $k$ is $\mathcal{C}_k = B(\mu_k, R_{99})$. The separation above makes the cores
disjoint. For this mixture the exact field $F_\star$ is available in closed form (Sec.~B), whereas
a trained network gives the learned field $F_\theta$. Every sampler we consider is deterministic, so
a seed $z \sim \gamma = \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$ has a single sample $x = \mathcal{G}(z)$, and we define
its \emph{outcome}
\begin{equation}
    o(z) =
    \begin{cases}
        k,  & \mathcal{G}(z) \in \mathcal{C}_k, \\
        -1, & \mathcal{G}(z) \notin \bigcup_{k} \mathcal{C}_k \quad \text{(hallucination)}.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $o(z) = k$ exactly when $z$ lies in the pulled-back core
$\mathcal{K}_k = \mathcal{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_k)$, and hallucinating seeds form the band outside
every $\mathcal{K}_k$. Predicting the outcome from the seed alone is therefore a $(K+1)$-class
classification problem in seed space, whose decision boundaries are the pulled-back boundaries
$\partial\mathcal{K}_k$.


\subsection{Anchor Classification}
\label{app:anchor-class}

\paragraph{Pullback anchors.}
The pulled-back cores have no closed form, so we describe them with \emph{anchors}: seeds whose
outcome is known because they were obtained by pullback of labelled points in data space
(Algorithm~1). For a budget of $b$ anchors per mode, we draw $b$ points uniformly in each mode
core $\mathcal{C}_k$ (label $k$) and $b/2$ points in the hallucination band
$R_{99} < \|x - \mu_k\| \le R_{99} + 2\sigma_0$ around it (label $-1$). Each point is written as
$x = \mu_k + \rho\, v$, with a direction $v = g / \|g\|$, $g \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$, which is
uniform on the unit sphere, and a radius $\rho$ drawn with $U \sim U[0, 1]$ as
\begin{equation}
    \rho = R_{99}\, U^{1/d} \quad \text{(core)}, \qquad
    \rho = R_{99} + 2\sigma_0\, U \quad \text{(band)}.
\end{equation}
The exponent $1/d$ makes the core points uniform in volume. The band points are uniform in radius,
which keeps them spread across the band in high dimension, where a volume-uniform draw would
concentrate them at its outer edge. The band points supply hallucination labels directly outside
the core boundaries, where the pulled-back boundaries $\partial\mathcal{K}_k$ lie.

The labelled points are pulled back to seed space by integrating the exact field $F_\star$ in
reverse, from $\tau = 0$ to $\tau = 1$, in $T$ steps (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}, top). For DDIM,
each reverse step is a DDIM update whose noise prediction averages the exact $\epsilon$ at both ends
of the step, a Heun-type correction that makes the pullback second order; the learned DDIM sampler
itself uses the plain first-order update. For flow matching, the exact velocity field is integrated
in reverse with Heun's method. Pullback never queries the learned model.

\paragraph{Reference outcomes.}
The test set consists of $50{,}000 \cdot K$ seeds $z \sim \gamma$, labelled by their outcome under
the learned endpoint map $\mathcal{G}_\theta$ (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}, bottom). The anchors are
built from $F_\star$ and the reference outcomes from $F_\theta$, so the evaluation measures how well
the pulled-back cores of the exact field predict the behaviour of the trained sampler. The number
of pullback steps $T$ affects only the anchors.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report the accuracy over the $K + 1$ outcome classes, the F1 score macro-averaged over the $K$
mode classes, and the F1 score of the hallucination class. Hallucinations are rare (about $1\%$ of
seeds, Table~S1), so accuracy is dominated by seeds inside the pulled-back cores, and hallucination
F1 is the relevant measure of how well the hallucination band is recovered. All results are means
over three runs, each with independent centres, network initialisation and test seeds.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
An experiment is specified by the sampler, the mixture variant, $K$, $d$, the number of pullback
steps $T$ and the anchor budget $b$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:app-protocol} gives the procedure for one
configuration, and Table~\ref{tab:app-grid} lists every configuration we evaluate; the values of
the constants in the algorithms are given in Tables~\ref{tab:app-grid}, \ref{tab:app-scorenet}
and~\ref{tab:app-classifiers}. The learned
field and the reference outcomes depend on neither $T$ nor $b$, so they are shared across both. In the result tables, each row is a pair $(K, d)$, and
each classifier has one sub-column per value of $T$; the heatmaps show the three metrics over the
$(K, d)$ grid, one panel per classifier and metric.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Evaluation protocol for one configuration (sampler, mixture variant, $K$, $d$, $T$, $b$).}
\label{alg:app-protocol}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=appcode]
# mu[0..K-1] : mode centres, R99 : mode-core radius, gap : minimum centre separation
# F_star / F_theta : exact / learned field, G_star / G_theta : their endpoint maps
# T : pullback steps, n, m : core and band anchors per mode, w : band width
# N_train : training samples, N_test : test seeds per mode, n_runs : runs
# eta_max : retraining threshold on the hallucination rate, s_re : retraining step factor

def outcome(x, mu, R99):                          # k if x in C_k, -1 = hallucination
    D = cdist(x, mu)
    return where(min(D, axis=1) <= R99, argmin(D, axis=1), -1)

for run in range(n_runs):
    mu, w_mix = draw_centres(K, d, gap), draw_weights(K)
    F_theta   = train_field(sample_gmm(mu, w_mix, N_train))
    if hallucination_rate(F_theta) > eta_max:
        F_theta = train_field(sample_gmm(mu, w_mix, N_train), steps_factor=s_re)

    z     = randn(N_test * K, d)                  # test seeds
    o_ref = outcome(G_theta(z), mu, R99)          # reference outcomes

    Z, y  = pullback(mu, R99, n, m, w, F_star, T) # Alg. 1
    for clf in classifiers:
        o_hat = clf.fit(Z, y).predict(z)
        scores[run, clf] = metrics(o_hat, o_ref)  # accuracy, mode F1, hallucination F1

return mean(scores, axis=0)
\end{lstlisting}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Diffusion Model on GMM}
\label{app:details-diffusion}

\paragraph{Samplers.}
We consider two generative processes, and three further solvers for one of them. All samplers are
deterministic instances of Eq.~(1), so each seed has a single outcome.

\textit{DDIM.} A variance-preserving diffusion,
$x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\, \epsilon$, with a linear schedule
$\beta_t \in [10^{-4}, 0.02]$ over 500 steps. The network is trained with the standard
$\epsilon$-prediction objective and sampled with the deterministic DDIM update, i.e.\ the Euler
discretisation of the probability-flow ODE (Song et al., 2021a;b).

\textit{Flow matching.} The optimal-transport conditional path of Lipman et al.~(2023),
$x_\tau = (1 - (1 - \sigma_{\min})\,\tau)\, z + \tau\, x$ with $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$ and
$\sigma_{\min} = 10^{-4}$. The network regresses the conditional velocity and is sampled with 500
Euler steps. As in Sec.~3.1, we write $z$ for the seed and $x = \mathcal{G}_\theta(z)$ for the
sample of both processes.

\textit{Heun, RK45 and DPM-Solver++(2M).} The trained DDIM network, integrated on the same
500-step grid with a different solver: Heun's method (second order, 2 network evaluations per
step), fixed-step Dormand--Prince RK45 (fifth order, 6 evaluations per step) and DPM-Solver++(2M)
(Lu et al., 2022; second-order multistep, 1 evaluation per step). These samplers are not retrained:
they share the DDIM network and anchors, and differ from DDIM only in the reference outcomes. They
therefore isolate the effect of the discretisation on the pulled-back cores.

\paragraph{Network.}
All learned fields use the residual MLP of Figure~\ref{fig:app-scorenet}: four pre-LayerNorm
residual blocks with LeakyReLU(0.2) activations and a sinusoidal time embedding. The width is 256
for $d \le 64$ and 1024 for $d \ge 128$. Models are trained with a mean-squared-error objective
using Adam and an exponential moving average of the weights (Table~\ref{tab:app-scorenet}). A
model whose hallucination rate on $10^4$ probe seeds exceeds $1.5\%$ is retrained once with
$1.7\times$ the number of steps.

\subsection{Algorithm Details}
\label{app:algo-details}

We compare four classifiers that map a seed to one of the $K + 1$ outcomes, given in
Algorithms~\ref{alg:app-knn}--\ref{alg:app-param-train} and configured as in
Table~\ref{tab:app-classifiers}. kNN, the quadratic classifier and the polar classifier are fit on
the core and band anchors of Appendix~\ref{app:anchor-class}. Weighted kNN (w-kNN) builds its own
anchors from the mode cores only and detects hallucinations by low confidence. The quadratic and
polar classifiers differ only in their logits (Algorithms~\ref{alg:app-quadratic}
and~\ref{alg:app-polar}); both are trained with cross-entropy over the $K + 1$ classes, as
ensembles of three models whose log-probabilities are summed (Algorithm~\ref{alg:app-param-train}).

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{kNN: seed $\to$ outcome.}
\label{alg:app-knn}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=appcode]
# Z, y : pulled-back anchors, labels in {-1, 0..K-1};  z : test seeds
# k : neighbours, eps : numerical floor
Y     = one_hot(y + 1, K + 1)                     # column 0 = hallucination
idx   = topk(cdist(z, Z), k, largest=False)       # k nearest anchors
lp    = log(mean(Y[idx], axis=1) + eps)           # log vote shares
m     = lp[:, 0] - max(lp[:, 1:], axis=1)         # hallucination margin
o_hat = where(m > 0, -1, argmax(lp[:, 1:], axis=1))
\end{lstlisting}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Weighted kNN (w-kNN) over mode-only anchors: seed $\to$ outcome.}
\label{alg:app-altered}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=appcode]
# mu, R99, F_star, T : as in Alg. 1;  z : test seeds
# n : anchors per mode, S : spheres per mode, r_max : largest sphere radius (in R99)
# w_min : weight of the outermost sphere, k : neighbours, temp : softmax temperature
# n_cal : calibration seeds, [q_lo, q_hi], n_q : quantile range and count for the threshold
P, y, om = [], [], []
for c in range(K):
    for j in range(1, S + 1):
        P  += [mu[c] + (j / S) * r_max * R99 * unit_vectors(n // S, d)]
        y  += [c]
        om += [1 - (1 - w_min) * j / S]           # linear decay to w_min
Za = transport(P, F_star, T)                      # pullback step of Alg. 1

def vote(z):
    idx  = topk(cdist(z, Za), k, largest=False)
    s    = sum(om[idx] * one_hot(y[idx], K), 1) / sum(om[idx], 1)
    p    = softmax(s / temp)
    conf = 1 - entropy(p) / log(K)
    return p, conf

# calibration on seeds labelled by the exact endpoint map
z_cal = randn(n_cal, d)
o_cal = outcome(G_star(z_cal), mu, R99)
_, c  = vote(z_cal)
theta = argmax_over(quantiles(c, q_lo .. q_hi, n_q),
                    lambda th: hall_f1(c < th, o_cal == -1))

p, conf = vote(z)
o_hat   = where(conf < theta, -1, argmax(p, axis=1))
\end{lstlisting}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Quadratic classifier: logits of a seed.}
\label{alg:app-quadratic}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=appcode]
# z : seeds (N, d);  output : K + 1 logits
def forward(z):
    iu  = triu_indices(d, d)                      # pairs i <= j
    psi = concat([z, z[:, iu[0]] * z[:, iu[1]]])  # d + d(d+1)/2 features
    return Linear[d + d * (d + 1) // 2 -> K + 1](psi)   # column 0 = hallucination
\end{lstlisting}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Polar classifier: logits of a seed.}
\label{alg:app-polar}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=appcode]
# z : seeds (N, d);  h : hidden size, p : polynomial degree;  output : K + 1 logits
def forward(z):
    r0  = sqrt(d)                                 # typical seed radius
    r   = norm(z, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    rs  = r - r0                                  # radial offset
    u   = z / r                                   # direction
    q   = Linear[d + 1 -> h]([u * sqrt(r0), rs]) / sqrt(r0 + 1)

    phi = concat([q ** i for i in range(1, p + 1)])
    l   = Linear[p * h -> K](phi)                 # mode logits

    t1, t2 = top2(l)
    l_h = c + a * rs - exp(log_beta) * (t1 - t2)  # hallucination logit
    return concat([l_h, l])
\end{lstlisting}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Training and prediction of the quadratic and polar classifiers.}
\label{alg:app-param-train}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=appcode]
# Z, y : pulled-back anchors;  arch in {quadratic, polar};  z : test seeds
# E : ensemble size, B : batch size, lr, wd : learning rate and weight decay
# e_min, s_min : minimum epochs and minimum optimiser steps
nets = []
for e in range(E):
    net    = Classifier(d, K, arch, seed=e)
    epochs = max(e_min, ceil(s_min / (len(Z) // B)))
    train(net, Z, y + 1, loss=cross_entropy, epochs=epochs, batch=B,
          opt=AdamW(lr=lr, weight_decay=wd), sched="one-cycle")
    nets += [net]

lp    = sum(log_softmax(net(z)) for net in nets)
m     = lp[:, 0] - max(lp[:, 1:], axis=1)         # hallucination margin
o_hat = where(m > 0, -1, argmax(lp[:, 1:], axis=1))
\end{lstlisting}
\end{algorithm}

The polar classifier uses the fact that $\|z\|$ concentrates around $\sqrt{d}$ for $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$,
so the outcome depends mainly on the direction $u$. Its hallucination logit grows as the two
largest mode logits approach each other, so hallucinations are predicted between two pulled-back
cores, in a band whose width depends on the radius. This matches the geometry of Sec.~3.3, where
the class boundaries arise from the contact of neighbouring cores.

\subsection{Additional MNIST Results}
\label{app:mnist}

We test whether pullback predicts outcomes on real data, whose distribution is only approximately
a Gaussian mixture (Sec.~5.3). We train an autoencoder whose latent space is shaped to resemble the
mixtures of Appendix~\ref{app:details_gmm}, run a latent diffusion model in that space, and predict
the outcome of its seeds as before, on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10.

\paragraph{Latent space.}
Each dataset has $K = 10$ classes, and the latent dimension is $d = 32$. We train a convolutional
autoencoder with encoder $E$ and decoder $D_{\text{dec}}$. The encoder sees only images, but the
loss uses class labels to impose the geometry of Appendix~\ref{app:details_gmm} on the latents
$x = E(\text{image})$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L} = \; & \|\text{image} - D_{\text{dec}}(x + \xi)\|^2
    + \lambda_{\text{sep}}\, \mathbb{E}_{y_a \ne y_b} \max\!\Bigl(0, \tfrac{D_{\text{sep}} - \|x_a - x_b\|}{\sigma_0}\Bigr)^{2} \\
    & + \frac{\lambda_{\text{cov}}}{K} \sum_{c} \Bigl\| \tfrac{\mathrm{Cov}_c(x)}{\sigma_0^2} - I_d \Bigr\|_F^2
    + \frac{\lambda_{\text{norm}}}{K} \sum_{c} \max\!\Bigl(0, \tfrac{\|m_c\| - R}{\sigma_0}\Bigr)^{2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\xi \sim \mathcal{N}(0, (0.15\,\sigma_0)^2 I_d)$, $\mathrm{Cov}_c$ and $m_c$ are the latent
covariance and mean of class $c$, and $\lambda_{\text{sep}} = 5$, $\lambda_{\text{cov}} = 2$ and
$\lambda_{\text{norm}} = 1$. The constants are those of the Gaussian mixtures: $\sigma_0 = 0.1$,
$R = 2\sqrt{d/2}$ and $D_{\text{sep}} = 3R_{99}$. The terms ask for latents of different classes to
be at least $D_{\text{sep}}$ apart, for each class to have covariance $\sigma_0^2 I_d$, and for the
class means to lie within radius $R$; the means themselves are not prescribed. Fashion-MNIST and
CIFAR-10 are trained with random flips and shifts, so that test images also fall inside their
clusters.

\paragraph{Fitted mixture and mode cores.}
The latent distribution is only approximately Gaussian, so we fit a mixture to the encoded training
set without labels: $k$-means with $K = 10$ gives the centres $\hat\mu_i$, the relative cluster
sizes give the weights $\hat w_i$, and the pooled within-cluster variance gives $\hat\sigma_0^2$.
$R_{99}$ is computed per cluster, and the largest value is used throughout. Labels are used only
afterwards, to name each cluster by its majority class. Because the latents do not fill the fitted
balls exactly, the mode cores are enlarged to $\hat{\mathcal{C}}_k = B(\hat\mu_k, 1.25\,R_{99})$:
the outcome of a seed is $k$ if its sample lies in $\hat{\mathcal{C}}_k$, and $-1$ otherwise. A
\emph{hallucination} is therefore a latent sample outside every enlarged core.

\paragraph{Diffusion model.}
We use DDIM with 200 steps and the network of Figure~\ref{fig:app-scorenet} (width 256), trained
on the encoded training images rather than on samples of the fitted mixture. We deliberately train
it for a tenth of the budget of Appendix~\ref{app:details-diffusion},
$3{,}000\,(1 + d/16)(1 + K/16)$ steps, without retraining: a fully trained model hallucinates on
about $0.3\%$ of seeds, too few to evaluate detection. The hallucinations of this model are mostly
near misses, whose samples land just outside a core.

\paragraph{Anchors, reference outcomes and evaluation.}
Anchors are sampled as in Appendix~\ref{app:anchor-class}, with $b$ points in each enlarged core
and $b/2$ in the surrounding band of width $2\hat\sigma_0$. Unlike the Gaussian mixtures, the exact
field of the data is unknown, so the anchors are pulled back by inverting the \emph{learned} DDIM
sampler, with two fixed-point iterations per step, so that sampling from an anchor seed returns the
anchor exactly; the w-kNN threshold is calibrated on seeds labelled by the same model. The test set
consists of $100{,}000$ seeds, labelled by their outcome under $\mathcal{G}_\theta$. The classifiers
and metrics are those of Appendices~\ref{app:anchor-class} and~\ref{app:algo-details}. We add one
baseline, the \emph{exact field of the fitted mixture}: each test seed is mapped with
$\hat{\mathcal{G}}_\star$, and its outcome is compared with that under $\mathcal{G}_\theta$. As an
independent check, a CNN classifier trained on each dataset reads the decoded samples, to verify
that a sample in core $\hat{\mathcal{C}}_k$ decodes to an image of the class of cluster $k$.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:app-mnist} summarises the procedure and Table~\ref{tab:app-mnist} gives the
values of its constants.

\paragraph{Decoded samples.}
To show what the predicted seed regions look like, we draw $10{,}000$ fresh seeds, fit each
classifier on the anchors at the largest budget, and predict the outcome of every seed. For each
predicted class, we run the learned sampler on the first 10 seeds whose sample clearly belongs to
it and decode the sample with $D_{\text{dec}}$: a cluster's samples must lie within $0.7\,R_{99}$ of
its centre, and a hallucination's beyond $1.25\,R_{99}$ from every centre. For each classifier we
also report the precision and recall of its hallucination predictions on these seeds, and the
distance of the flagged samples to their nearest cluster centre.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Evaluation protocol in image latent spaces, for one dataset and anchor budget $b$.}
\label{alg:app-mnist}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=appcode]
# images, labels : one dataset with K classes;  d : latent dimension
# kappa : core enlargement, n, m : core and band anchors per mode, n_fp : fixed-point iterations
# S_train : training steps of the learned field, N_test : test seeds, n_runs : runs

for run in range(n_runs):
    E, Dec    = train_autoencoder(images, labels, d)    # loss L above
    X         = E(images)                               # latent training set
    mu, w, s0 = kmeans_mixture(X, K)                    # fitted mixture, no labels
    R         = kappa * max(R99_per_cluster(X, mu))     # enlarged mode-core radius
    F_theta   = train_field(X, steps=S_train)

    z      = randn(N_test, d)                           # test seeds
    o_ref  = outcome(G_theta(z), mu, R)                 # reference outcomes
    o_base = outcome(G_star_fit(z), mu, R)              # exact field of the fitted mixture
    scores[run, "exact"] = metrics(o_base, o_ref)

    Z, y = pullback(mu, R, n, m, 2 * s0, invert(G_theta, n_fp))
    for clf in classifiers:
        o_hat = clf.fit(Z, y).predict(z)
        scores[run, clf] = metrics(o_hat, o_ref)

return mean(scores, axis=0)
\end{lstlisting}
\end{algorithm}
